The pain point

Falcon is the Arab world's flagship open model — yet it learned almost everything from English. Its Arabic users deserve answers whose logic can be checked, not just trusted.

We do not make Falcon know more Arabic. We make what Falcon says in Arabic checkable — step by step, by a proof kernel, with the result shown to the user.

What we built

FalconVerifier is a verification layer that sits between Falcon and the user. Every reasoning step and the final answer are translated into Lean 4 propositions; the Lean kernel proves each one or its negation; refuted claims are explained back to Falcon in its own language and it revises. The translation is deterministic for the Arabic fragments we cover (arithmetic, quantifier logic, relations and counting) and falls back to Falcon-H1-Arabic-34B — Falcon formalizing Falcon — with a round-trip faithfulness audit elsewhere. Anything the kernel cannot decide is reported unknown, never guessed.

Measured on our Arabic benchmark with the weak Falcon-H1-Arabic-3B student: 244 problems, 48% → 76% accuracy, 92% of wrong answers caught by Lean, 0 correct answers broken. Every number links to its trace on the Benchmark page.

How to use it

  1. In the browser. Open the Verifier, type a math or logic question in Arabic or English (or click an example), press Verify with Lean 4. Watch each of Falcon's steps receive a kernel verdict; expand “Lean 4 source” to see exactly what was proved; download the assurance trace as JSON.
  2. As an API. One call wraps any Falcon deployment:
    POST /api/solve {"problem": "…", "student_model": "falcon-h1-arabic-3b-instruct"}
    returns the final answer, per-step propositions, verdicts, feedback and the assurance score. Stream rounds live with /api/solve/stream.
  3. From the shell / your stack. pip install -e . then falconverifier solve "…", falconverifier bench for the closed-loop benchmark, falconverifier export-dpo to turn refuted→verified pairs into training data. A Docker image with Lean 4 + Mathlib is published to GHCR.

Pregroup grammar, briefly

A pregroup grammar (Lambek, 1999) assigns each word a type built from a few atoms — here π (subject), o (object), s₀ (statement) — and two adjoints, left xˡ and right xʳ. Parsing is nothing but cancellation:

xˡ · x → 1     x · xʳ → 1
a sentence is grammatical ⇔ the product of its word types reduces to s₀

There is no search, no probabilities and no dictionary of meanings: a derivation is a finite sequence of cancellations, so checking one is mechanical — which is exactly why we could write the grammar in Lean and prove things about it.

Categorical grammar. Types with cancellation form a compact closed monoidal category; a derivation is a morphism. Meaning is a functor from that category into a semantic one — vector spaces in DisCoCat (Coecke, Sadrzadeh, Clark), Lean propositions here. A functor preserves structure, so the meaning of a sentence is assembled from the meanings of its words along the same wires the grammar used to parse it.

This is what lets the verifier work “without understanding the definitions”: the grammar fixes the shape of the proposition, the lexicon fixes the symbols, and the Lean kernel fixes the truth. No component has to know what a friend or a dirham is.

Why VSO makes Arabic hard to formalize

كَتَبَ أَحْمَدُ الدَّرْسَ
wrote · Ahmad · the-lesson — “Ahmad wrote the lesson.” Verb first (VSO), the default order of Classical and Modern Standard Arabic.
kataba : s₀ · oˡ · πˡ   ahmad : π   al-darsa : o
(s₀ · oˡ · πˡ) · π · o
→ s₀ · oˡ · o
→ s₀  ✓

In English (SVO) the verb sits between its arguments, so its type πʳ · s₀ · oˡ cancels one neighbour on each side. An Arabic verb comes first and must reach rightwards for both subject and object — two left adjoints stacked, oˡ · πˡ, in the reverse order of the words they consume. The bookkeeping is stricter, and the same verb needs a second type πʳ · s₀ · oˡ because Arabic also allows SVO (أحمدُ كتبَ الدرسَ).

Three concrete difficulties, and what we did.

1. Two word orders, one meaning. A formalizer that treats VSO and SVO as different structures produces different Lean terms for the same fact. We proved in Lean (vso_svo_same_meaning) that both derivations denote the same proposition, so the parser may normalize order safely.

2. Agreement lives in the verb. In VSO the verb precedes its subject and must already agree with it in gender (كتبَ vs كتبَتْ). A grammar with one subject atom cannot see this and accepts ungrammatical Arabic; we proved that too (coarse_accepts_bad_agreement) and showed that feature-indexed atoms π_m, π_f in the spirit of Bargelli–Lambek restore faithfulness. Coarse grammars silently lose meaning; that is the failure mode an LLM formalizer also has.

3. Morphology and script. One token can pack preposition + noun + pronoun (ولأصدقائه “and to his friends”), Eastern ٣٤٢ and Western 342 digits coexist, and numbers flow left-to-right inside right-to-left text. Each is a place where a translation can quietly change the claim; the deterministic fragments handle them by construction, and everything else is audited by a round-trip check before it may be called verified.
