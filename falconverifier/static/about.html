Falcon is the region's model and most of its users write Arabic. Arabic is a minority language in its training data, so its Arabic math and logic are weaker — but the truth of a math or logic claim does not depend on the language it was written in. FalconVerifier translates what Falcon says into Lean 4 and lets the kernel decide.

The loop

  1. Falcon answersThe student model (Falcon-H1 3B/7B) answers in Arabic or English, with its reasoning steps.
  2. FormalizeEach step and the final answer become Lean 4 propositions — deterministically for the Arabic pregroup fragments, otherwise via Falcon-H1-Arabic-34B with a round-trip faithfulness audit.
  3. Lean 4 decidesThe kernel tries to prove P and ¬P. Only a proof counts.
  4. TeachRefuted claims turn into plain-language feedback (with the Lean theorem or a concrete counter-model) sent back to Falcon.
  5. AssureFalcon revises; every round, every proposition and every verdict is kept in a downloadable assurance trace.

What a verdict means

verified
Lean proved the claim.
refuted
Lean proved its negation.
unknown
Neither side could be proved — we say so.
ill-formed
The proposition did not type-check.
unverified_premise
A world-knowledge premise (“Mecca is in Saudi Arabia”). Lean has no fact base: the premise is neither confirmed nor refuted; only the inference drawn from it is checked.
skipped
Descriptive text, no checkable claim.

The assurance score is the share of Falcon's claims the kernel actually proved — it is a verifiability measure, not model confidence. Assurance is the product; correction is what happens when assurance finds something.

Why Arabic

Arabic in Falcon shows the low baseline (3B: 58% on our set, vs. 97% for 7B/English), Eastern and Western digits side by side, RTL text mixed with numbers, rich morphology (one word can carry a preposition, a noun and a pronoun), free VSO/SVO word order and connective ambiguity (إما … أو). These are exactly the places where meaning is lost — and exactly what a formal target removes. Modern Standard Arabic is highly regular, so a pregroup grammar can parse our arithmetic, quantifier-logic and relation fragments deterministically; we proved in Lean that VSO and SVO readings have the same meaning, so word order is normalized safely.

Pregroup grammar, in one paragraph

Every word gets a type built from n (noun/number), s (sentence), q (question) and adjoints xˡ, xʳ. Adjacent types cancel: xˡ·x → 1, x·xʳ → 1. A sentence is well-formed iff its type product reduces to s. Categorically the grammar is a compact closed monoidal category and the translation to meaning is a structure-preserving functor — here into Lean propositions. That is why the checker never has to “understand” a definition: the grammar fixes the shape, the kernel fixes the truth.

Example: five friends

في غرفة ٥ أشخاص، يقول كل واحد منهم إن ٣ من الباقين أصدقاؤه. هل يمكن أن يكون الجميع صادقين؟
Five people are in a room; each says three of the others are their friends. Can they all be telling the truth?

Falcon (3B) answers لا (no). The relation fragment types صديق as a symmetric binary relation and asks Lean whether a 3-regular graph on 5 vertices exists.

∀ f : Fin 5 → Fin 5 → Bool, ¬ FalconVerifier.Regular f 3
-- proved via the handshake lemma:
--   Σ degree = 2 · |edges|, but 5 · 3 = 15 is odd

No enumeration of 210 graphs, no LLM judging the answer: the kernel proves the claim Falcon made, so the final answer is verified. Had Falcon said نعم, the same theorem refutes it and becomes the feedback.

What this does not do

  • It does not make Falcon smarter in general — only claims inside a decidable fragment get a proof.
  • Proof questions, open “why” questions and word problems that need equation modelling are reported unknown/skipped, not guessed.
  • The LLM formalizer can be unfaithful; our fragments avoid it where possible and a structural round-trip check downgrades mismatches to unknown rather than raising a false alarm.
